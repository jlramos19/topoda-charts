Player fantasy

Build a label that leaves a mark.

You operate at the label level. Sign and develop Creators, support Acts, decide where money and attention go, set production and release priorities, and intervene when a decision belongs to the executive layer.

The simulation handles the work happening around those decisions. Your job is to read the state, make commitments, advance time, see what changed, and decide what comes next.

What you do

Roster. Releases. Market. Legacy.

01

Build your roster. Sign and develop Creators and support Acts.

02

Set priorities. Allocate budgets, schedules, constraints, and approval boundaries.

03

Guide releases. Shape production and release strategy without manually performing Creator labor.

04

Read the market. Follow audiences, Trends, critics, charts, finances, and reputation.

05

Build Legacy. Careers, releases, relationships, and world events accumulate over time.

Why outcomes make sense

Results have causes.

The game is designed around inspectable outcomes. If a release succeeds, stalls, finds a new audience, loses momentum, or changes an Act's career, you should be able to see the important reasons behind that result.

Routine play stays compact. Deeper detail is there when you want to inspect the chain underneath.

Gaia

A music industry inside a future Earth.

Gaia gives the simulation cities, nations, communities, institutions, media, culture, economy, and history. Music enters places with their own local context instead of an abstract global market.

The Twenty-four Hundreds contributes story, characters, and history to that same world through game-native narrative surfaces.
